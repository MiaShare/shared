PLAT-31 · UX review

Mia can now change a payment schedule

A storyboard of the new confirm-before-execute “change payment schedule” tool in Mia, the school-side AI assistant. Captured end-to-end in the real running app, driven by the real language model. Five flows: one golden path, one single-date variant, one auto-applying start-date change, and two guardrails (refusal + cancel). Please review the UX and the basic flow logic.

How the feature works (read this first)

A school admin opens Mia from the sparkles “AI Assistant” button in the top nav and asks, in plain English, to move a student’s payment due dates. Mia finds the contract, prepares the change, and renders a confirmation card — the new UI element under review. Nothing is committed until the admin presses Confirm. The model is built to be safe by default:

1
ProposeMia drafts the change and shows a before/after card. No data has changed yet.
2
ConfirmAdmin reviews and confirms (or cancels). Cancel commits nothing.
3
Borrower approvesFor most change types the student must approve in their portal before it applies.
!
ExceptionsA start-date change applies immediately (no borrower step). A contract with a pending change is refused.

Golden path

1 · Bulk shift — move the whole upcoming schedule

Student Takeela · contract 48602 · payment plan currently due on the 13th, with one past-due payment. Goal: move every upcoming payment to the 15th and leave paid / past-due payments alone.

Starting schedule (before)

Interaction
Admin opens Takeela’s contract and the Payment Schedule tab in the school portal.
Showing
The “before” state — all 12 payments due on the 13th; payments 1–2 are paid, payment 3 (May 13) is past due. This is what we’re about to shift.

Asking Mia, in plain English

Interaction
Admin clicks the sparkles AI Assistant button (top nav) and types: “Move Takeela’s remaining payments to the 15th of each month.”
Showing
How an admin requests a schedule change — no form, just a sentence.

The confirmation card (the new element)

Interaction
Mia looks up the contract, prepares the change, and renders the confirmation card.
Showing
A row-by-row before/after preview: payments 1–3 stay unchanged (Paid / Paid / Past Due), payments 4–12 move 13th → 15th. It states past-due payments are left alone and that the borrower will approve. Nothing is committed yet.

Confirmed → request sent to the student

Interaction
Admin clicks Confirm on the card.
Showing
Mia confirms the request was sent to the borrower to approve — it does not apply until the student accepts (30-day expiry).

Borrower review (student portal)

Interaction
Sign out, log in as the student (Takeela), open Contract Updates in the student portal.
Showing
The borrower’s side — the proposed before/after with a required “I have reviewed…” checkbox that gates the Approve button.

Borrower approves

Interaction
Student checks the acknowledgement box and clicks Approve change.
Showing
The post-approval state — the change is now Completed and effective Jun 15, 2026.

Final schedule (after)

Interaction
Back in the admin portal, reload Takeela’s Payment Schedule.
Showing
The result — upcoming payments now land on the 15th; the past-due 5/13 payment was deliberately left untouched.

Auto-apply

3 · Start date — applies immediately, no borrower step

Student Priya · interest-bearing (RIC) contract 48607. A start-date change re-anchors the whole schedule and, unlike the others, takes effect right away.

Interest-bearing schedule (before)

Interaction
Open Priya’s RIC contract → Payment Schedule.
Showing
The “before” — a daily-interest schedule starting Jul 1, 2026, with each payment split into principal + interest.

Asking to change the start date

Interaction
Open Mia: “Change the start date of Priya’s plan to September 1, 2026.”
Showing
A different kind of change — re-anchoring the entire repayment start.

Card with the “applies immediately” warning

Mia prepares the change; the card is scrolled to show the full table and the callout.
Showing
All 12 payments re-anchored (Jul 1 → Sep 1, …) and the key UX difference: a yellow callout stating this applies immediately and does not need borrower approval. This is the one flow without a student step — worth a close UX look.

Schedule regenerated (after)

Showing
The schedule has regenerated starting Sep 1, 2026 — applied instantly, with no borrower approval in the loop.
Guardrail

4 · Refusal — a change is already pending

Student Marcus · contract 48606, which already has a pending change awaiting the borrower. Mia should refuse rather than stack a second request.

Mia declines and explains

Interaction
Open Mia: “Move Marcus’s payments to the 1st.”
Showing
The guardrail in action — no card is shown. Mia explains the contract already has a pending change waiting on the borrower and offers options (wait for the student, or have the team resolve the existing request). Nothing is created.
Guardrail

5 · Cancel — back out before committing

Student Devon · contract 48604. The admin gets a valid card but decides not to proceed.

Card rendered — the escape hatch

Interaction
Open Mia: “Move Devon’s payments to the 5th.” A valid bulk-shift card appears.
Showing
The card with both Cancel and Confirm — the admin’s chance to back out before anything is committed.

Cancelled — nothing changes

Interaction
Admin clicks Cancel.
Showing
Mia acknowledges “action cancelled.” No request was created and Devon’s schedule is untouched.
